## Q1 (Part A). The Japanese medical device companies that became global leaders: share, overseas %, margin, and how they won

### Takeaway
Japan's winners share four traits:
- They are pure-plays or near-pure-plays: Olympus sold cameras, scientific and orthopedics; Terumo, Sysmex and Asahi Intecc were always focused.
- Most sales come from overseas: Olympus >85%, Terumo ~80%, Sysmex ~88%.
- Each built its own overseas sales and service network early, often by buying distributors (Olympus from 1964/68, Sysmex 1995–98) or by acquisitions abroad (Terumo).
- A large share of revenue recurs from consumables, service or disposables (Olympus service is ~35% of its endoscopy business).

Their operating margins (~10–25%) are well above the ~4–7% that Japan's imaging divisions earned, whether under Toshiba or Hitachi or later under Canon.

### Inferences
- The winners' common features:
  - Each won a defined clinical niche, either by inventing it or co-developing it with doctors (gastrocamera → EMR/ESD; hematology automation).
  - Each built its own overseas sales and service channels decades before China mattered, and sells mostly abroad (80–88%).
  - Each has a recurring stream of service, reagents or disposables.
  - Each is run as a focused company. Olympus deliberately became a pure-play between 2021 and 2024.
- Margins rank in the same order: Asahi Intecc (~25%), Terumo (~16–19% adj.), HOYA Life Care (~19% in Q4) and Olympus (~14% adj.) sit well above Japanese imaging. Toshiba Medical earned ~6% and Canon Medical 4–7%.
- Even Japan's winners are being pushed back in China (Olympus, Sysmex and HOYA endoscopes all cite China). Domestic substitution in China is not limited to capital imaging.

## Q2 (Part A). Is the common pattern "focused specialists with recurring consumables won; conglomerate heavy-electric divisions selling capital equipment lost"? Academic or analyst work for or against

### Takeaway
I found no single academic paper that states this hypothesis outright. What exists is consistent with it but different in kind:
- Japanese government material frames the split as diagnostic (surplus) vs therapeutic (deficit), and as small firms without global networks.
- The USITC (2007) notes Japanese firms concentrate on imaging and commodity supplies.
- Trade-press profiles show the conglomerate imaging units had dispersed structures and ~5–7% margins.

The hypothesis is best treated as a well-supported synthesis, not as an established finding. One counter-example weakens the "conglomerate" half: Fujifilm, a conglomerate, is a GI-endoscope #2 and a successful buyer of Hitachi imaging.

### Cited Findings
- METI's Vision for the Medical Device Industry 2024 [GOV] — [METI Vision 2024 (EN)](https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/mono_info_service/healthcare/iryou/downloadfiles/pdf/iryoukikisangyouvision2024/Vision_for_the_Medical_Device_Industry_2024.pdf); [Japanese version](https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/mono_info_service/healthcare/iryou/downloadfiles/pdf/iryoukikisangyouvision2024/iryoukikisangyouvision2024.pdf):
  - Japan's share of global medical-device production fell from ~22.2% (1990) to ~5.0% (2018). I saw this via a search excerpt; the PDF returned 403.
  - Most domestic market growth is absorbed by imports.
  - The proposed strategy: large firms with sales networks should absorb startup innovation, government should help firms build US channels, and startups should get support.
- METI study-group minutes say even Japan's top device firms are not in the global top 10. The stated weaknesses are overseas networks, regulatory work, sales channels, capital and know-how, and a shortage of people who can run international trials [GOV] — [METI 研究会 議事要旨](https://www.meti.go.jp/shingikai/mono_info_service/medical_device/pdf/003_gijiyoshi.pdf).
- MHLW material: therapeutic devices run an import surplus with a negative competitiveness index, while diagnostic devices run an export surplus with a positive index [GOV] — [MHLW](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/shingi/2r9852000000yb0d-att/2r9852000000yb54.pdf).
  - An older MHLW paper says the index has fallen since 1990, and the US holds over half of therapeutic-device patents [GOV, 2003] — [MHLW 2003](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/shingi/2003/03/dl/s0331-5a1.pdf).
- Trade balance in 2024 [MEDIA relaying MHLW 薬事工業生産動態統計] — [Yakuji Nippo (revised)](https://www.yakuji.co.jp/?p=134015); [Yakuji Nippo (initial)](https://www.yakuji.co.jp/?p=128108):
  - Domestic production ¥2.664tn (−0.4%).
  - Imports ¥3.454tn (revised; ¥3.606tn first published).
  - Exports ¥1.145tn.
  - That gives a deficit of ~¥2.3tn (calc).
- JEF Japan Spotlight: Japan dominates diagnostic imaging and endoscopy but has low competitiveness in therapeutic devices, with a trade deficit of ~¥1tn/yr [essay; snippet only, PDF 403; date not confirmed] — [JEF 260th Cover Story 06](https://jef.or.jp/journal/pdf/260th_Cover_Story_06.pdf).
- USITC Pub. 3909 (2007) [GOV, US; snippet only, PDF 403] — [USITC pub3909](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/332/pub3909.pdf):
  - Japanese firms concentrate on medical imaging and commodity hospital supplies, while US and EU firms cover advanced cardiovascular devices.
  - It attributes Japan's lag partly to health and regulatory policy and to lower R&D and venture-capital investment.
- Industry overseas ratio: across 30 listed Japanese device companies, overseas sales were 55% (FY2013) → 59% (FY2014) [industry association] — [JFMDA mini-column No.220, 2015-10-15](https://www.jfmda.gr.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/ミニコラム_20151015_第220号.pdf).
- JAAME research paper frames device competitiveness around four capabilities: 技術革新力 (technology innovation), 規制対応力 (regulatory response), 市場理解力 (market understanding) and グローバル展開力 (global expansion) [academic/industry] — [JAAME MDSI paper 044 summary](https://www.jaame.or.jp/mdsi/pdf/activity/researchpaper/mdsirp044_summary.pdf).
- NRI (June 2025): global medtech is polarizing into "channel majors" (> US$3bn revenue) and "technology niches" (US$100–300m). It also flags the rise of Chinese firms over the past decade [MR/consulting] — [NRI 知的資産創造 2025-06](https://www.nri.com/jp/knowledge/publication/chitekishisan_202506/02.html).
- Hitachi as a conglomerate example (2016) [MEDIA] — [Nikkan Kogyo / newswitch 2016-04-02](https://newswitch.jp/p/4175):
  - Hitachi's "総合力" (breadth across the line) was #1 in Japanese imaging, but the business was dispersed across many small units.
  - Healthcare operating margin was 5.7%, with a target of 10% by FY2018 and service above 30% of sales.
  - The piece claimed global #1 in ultrasound and particle therapy.
  - Hitachi sold imaging to Fujifilm in 2019–21.
- Toshiba sold Toshiba Medical (~6% margin) to raise cash after its accounting scandal [MEDIA] — [Toyo Keizai](https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/108887).
- Benchmark: Siemens Healthineers' Imaging adjusted EBIT margin was 26.5% in Q3 FY2026 [FILING] — [Siemens Healthineers Q3 FY2026 release, 2026-07-31](https://corporate.webassets.siemens-healthineers.com/2637d4a46dbcd161/a3335cc16ed1/siemens-healthineers-Q3_FY2026_Earnings-Release-and-Financial-Results.pdf). Canon Medical ROS was 4.3–6.7% [FILING] — [Canon 2025 briefing](https://global.canon/ja/ir/conference/pdf/event2025med-j.pdf).
  - The definitions differ (adjusted EBIT vs ROS), so this is directional only.

### Inferences
- **Supported:** Japan's imaging makers were divisions of heavy-electric or electronics conglomerates (Toshiba, Hitachi). They earned single-digit margins and were sold when the parent needed cash or refocused. The sales came from parent-level events, not from a medical strategy.
- **Supported:** the winners were focused businesses with recurring revenue and direct overseas service.
- **Weaker than it looks:**
  - Imaging itself has a service tail, and Siemens and GE make imaging pay at 20%+ margins. So capital equipment alone does not explain the losers.
  - Scale, a global installed base and service density look more decisive. Canon Medical's own plan centres on a "global service business reform" and on US sales, which points at the gap.
- **Counter-example:** Fujifilm, a conglomerate, became #2 in GI endoscopes and bought Hitachi imaging. This suggests that business-unit focus and reinvestment priority matter more than the ownership form.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or RIETI paper testing "specialist + consumables vs conglomerate capital equipment" across Japanese firms was found. RIETI searches returned only MHLW and METI documents.
- Several key PDFs (METI Vision EN, JEF, USITC) were blocked with 403, so some points rest on search excerpts.
- No data found on the history of GE (GE Yokogawa) in Japan, or on Japanese home-market shares in CT and MRI by vendor. This is needed to test the "GE did it in Japan" analogy.

## Q3 (Part B). China's imaging challengers in 2026: United Imaging, Mindray, Neusoft, Wandong, Anke (revenue, overseas share, high-end products, domestic share vs GPS, margins, R&D)

### Takeaway
United Imaging (UIH) is the only Chinese imaging maker that is both scaling and moving up-market:
- 2025 revenue was RMB 13.8bn (+34%), with overseas ~25%.
- It holds FDA clearances for a whole-body 5T MRI (2024) and the total-body PET/CT uEXPLORER (2019).
- It has a photon-counting CT in clinical use (2026).
- By its own claims it is #1 in China in CT, MR and PET/CT new-installation value.

The rest of the field is being squeezed by VBP:
- Mindray's domestic revenue fell 23% in 2025.
- Wandong made its first loss since its 1997 listing.

By tender value, domestic brands held 73% of ≤64-slice CT but only 43% of ≥128-slice CT in 2025. The top tier is still foreign-majority, but the domestic share there rose from 6% in 2019.

### Cited Findings

**United Imaging (688271.SH)**
- 2025 [FILING, press release 2026-04-29] — [PR Newswire Asia](https://en.prnasia.com/releases/global/united-imaging-healthcare-releases-2025-annual-report-and-q1-2026-results-delivering-strong-growth-with-accelerated-global-expansion-531290.shtml):
  - Revenue RMB 13.80bn (+33.98%); attributable net profit RMB 1.87bn (+48.14%), a ~13.6% net margin (calc).
  - R&D investment RMB 2.62bn (+15.95%), ~19.0% of revenue (calc).
  - Overseas revenue RMB 3.43bn (+51.39%), ~24.9% of the total (calc).
  - Regional growth: Europe ~+50%, Asia-Pacific +40%, emerging markets +80%.

## Q4 (Part B). China's policy tools that Japan never had: VBP, domestic-first procurement, equipment renewal, anti-corruption, plus the EU retaliation that shapes overseas expansion

### Takeaway
China combines five tools, which add up to a full-stack industrial policy at both the buyer and the price level:
1. Explicit domestic-content rules in public procurement: the 2021 list, and from 2026-01-01 a national "本国产品标准" (domestic-product standard) with a 20% price deduction.
2. Centralized purchasing of large equipment, with mid/low-end price cuts often of 50–70%. It was formalized nationally in July 2026.
3. State-funded hospital equipment renewal: +25% equipment investment by 2027, financed by ultra-long special treasury bonds.
4. An anti-corruption drive (from mid-2023) that froze orders and hit incumbent foreign sales channels hardest.
5. Since 2025, a reciprocal EU–China exclusion from each other's medical-device tenders.

I found no evidence that Japan ever had equivalent tools (see Gaps).

### Cited Findings

**1. Domestic-first procurement**
- The 2021 Ministry of Finance (MOF) and Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) guideline on approving imported products in government procurement [GOV via MEDIA; official text not found] — [cn-healthcare 2021-10-13](https://www.cn-healthcare.com/articlewm/20211013/content-1273004.html); [ChinaBaogao](https://www.chinabaogao.com/market/202309/665892.html):
  - 137 device types must be 100% domestic; 12 at 75%, 24 at 50%, 5 at 25%.
  - MRI, PET/CT, PET/MR and DR are reported as 100% domestic for government procurement.
  - Provinces cut their lists of importable devices: Zhejiang from 232 to 195, Guangdong from 132 to 46.
  - Whether ordinary CT is on the 100% list is unconfirmed.
- 国办发〔2025〕34号 (State Council notice on domestic-product standards in government procurement): published 2025-09-30, effective 2026-01-01 [GOV] — [Xinhua 2025-09-30](https://www.news.cn/politics/20250930/606ada1c704444b588e811c82e4f064c/c.html); [gov.cn](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202509/content_7042972.htm).
  - Shanghai's implementing notice gives domestic products a 20% price deduction in evaluation when they compete against non-domestic ones [GOV] — [Shanghai Finance Bureau](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/cmsres/0c/0cdf478e41264ea6898b5073e2305473/3d98a37353aecf90f87e306cec8efe8f.pdf).
  - Bid templates now ask for a declaration of the cost share of domestically made components, key components and key processes [GOV tender doc] — [Shanghai procurement doc](https://jpg.zfcg.sh.gov.cn/sh-gov-open-doc/1108QZ/38a46e14-7043-4a2b-96bc-274530d8f69f.pdf).
  - Canon reads this as "国産の定義厳格化" (a stricter definition of domestic) [FILING] — [Canon 2025 briefing](https://global.canon/ja/ir/conference/pdf/event2025med-j.pdf).
- 财库〔2025〕19号 (MOF notice): for listed devices with budgets above RMB 45m, where an imported product is genuinely needed, EU firms must be excluded. Effective 2025-07-06. This is retaliation for the EU measure below [GOV via MEDIA] — [Malaya/Reuters](https://malaya.com.ph/business/shipping-transportation/china-retaliates-to-eu-ban-with-limits-on-medical-devices).

**2. VBP and centralized purchasing of imaging equipment**
- Early pilots (2021): Anhui first. Fujian's provincial CT/MRI centralized purchasing gave "average cuts above 50%"; a ≥1.5T MRI won at RMB 2.93m [MEDIA] — [cn-healthcare 2021-08-25](https://www.cn-healthcare.com/articlewm/20210825/content-1256856.html).
- 2025 non-centralized projects across 8 provinces (Ningxia, Xinjiang, Henan, Beijing, Sichuan, Jiangsu, Hubei, Heilongjiang) [MEDIA/MR, 动脉网 2025-06-27] — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/3353983022166660):
  - Price falls: ~39% of projects fell less than 50%, ~34% fell 50–70%, ~27% fell more than 70%.
  - County medical-consortium prices: 1.5T MR ~RMB 2m, 64-slice CT ~RMB 1m, whole-body ultrasound below RMB 0.5m. CT and MR prices are "close to production cost".
  - Zhejiang ultra-high-end CT tender:
    - GE Revolution Apex at RMB 15.937m each, down 21.3–37.1% versus 2024.
    - UIH uCT960 (320-row) at RMB 9.89m each, down 37.4–41.1%.
  - Siemens sold 9 photon-counting CTs in China in H1 2025 (the same as in 2024), with the average price ~RMB 3m lower.
- July 2026 national framework: 《关于进一步做好公立医疗卫生机构医用设备集中采购工作的通知》 (notice on centralized purchasing of equipment by public medical institutions), issued 2026-07-14 by the NHC, the National Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine and the National Disease Control Administration [GOV] — [Xinhua 2026-07-15](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260715/e4a29263b5dd4d56bcef2e5cad8400d6/c.html):
  - National level: Class A (甲类) large equipment, mainly high-end radiotherapy; proton and heavy-ion systems excluded for now.
  - Provincial level: Class B (乙类) equipment — PET/MR, PET/CT, laparoscopic surgical robots, conventional radiotherapy. Each province must run at least one round by end-2026 and then one each H1.
  - City level: at least one city pilot per province by June 2027 for other equipment.
  - The notice itself contains no domestic or import clause.

**3. Large-scale equipment renewal (2024–)**
- The State Council action plan of March 2024 targets medical equipment investment +25% by 2027 versus 2023 [GOV via MEDIA] — [China Securities Journal 2024-04-12](https://cs.com.cn/tzjj/jjdt/202404/t20240412_6401508.html).
  - Funding sources: ultra-long special treasury bonds, local fiscal funds and special bonds, with support capped at 40/60/80/80% of project cost (East/Central/West/Northeast).
  - The first batch was estimated at ~RMB 60bn [MEDIA] — [10jqka 2024-10-29](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20241029/c662894816.shtml).
- About RMB 200bn of special treasury bonds supported equipment renewal in 2026, after ~RMB 300bn arranged in July 2025 [MEDIA] — [China News 2026-08-06](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/08-06/10672800.shtml).
- Philips cited a "slow national renewal program" as a 2025 drag [MEDIA] — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/philips-sales-struggles-china-2025/740303/).

**4. Anti-corruption campaign (mid-2023 →)**
- A one-year campaign overseen by 10 departments delayed orders [MEDIA] — [In Vivo/Citeline](https://invivo.citeline.com/IV154705/China-Headwinds-Set-To-Persist-For-Imaging-Majors); [Medical Device Network](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/news/ge-healthcare-cuts-2024-revenue-growth-outlook-as-china-freeze-hits-hard/). Effects on the foreign majors:
  - Siemens Healthineers' China imaging sales fell by double digits in fiscal Q3 2024.
  - GE HealthCare cut its 2024 growth outlook from 4% to 1–2%.
  - Philips' China orders fell.
- Earlier history: in 2015, Chinese regulators investigated GE, Siemens and Philips units for bribing hospitals [MEDIA] — [IndustryWeek](https://www.industryweek.com/public-policy/ge-siemens-philips-units-under-bribery-probe-china-report).
- Canon's March 2025 view was that the purchase-freeze effect had "partly cleared" [FILING] — [Canon 2025 briefing](https://global.canon/ja/ir/conference/pdf/event2025med-j.pdf).

**5. EU retaliation that shapes Chinese firms' overseas path**
- Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1197, the first use of the International Procurement Instrument, excludes Chinese companies from EU public procurement of medical devices above €5m. The Commission found 87% of China's medical-device public contracts were subject to exclusionary practices [legal analysis/MEDIA] — [Baker McKenzie 2025-07-16](https://healthcarelifesciences.bakermckenzie.com/2025/07/16/the-eu-restricts-access-of-chinese-medical-devices-to-the-european-market/); [Euronews 2025-06-20](https://www.euronews.com/2025/06/20/eu-shuts-out-chinese-medical-suppliers-from-european-market-in-retaliatory-trade-move).

### Inferences
- The tools work in sequence:
  1. Domestic-content rules decide who may bid.
  2. Centralized purchasing decides the price.
  3. Renewal bonds decide the volume (and timing).
  4. Anti-corruption removed the incumbents' relationship-based sales channel.
- Effect on prices: VBP turned the low end into a cost-only game (CT and MR "close to production cost"; DR and ultrasound down more than 60%). Medians are still rising because the mix is shifting to ≥256-row and PCCT. So the price war now reaches into the high end (the Zhejiang tender) rather than being confined to the low end.
- The EU's €5m tender bar hits UIH's fastest-growing overseas region (Europe, ~+50% in 2025) in exactly the large hospital tenders where CT, MR and PET/CT sit. Chinese champions face a politically constrained export path, which is a key difference from Japan's 1960s–1990s expansion.

### Gaps
- The official MOF text for the 2021 list was not retrieved, and whether ordinary CT is at 100% domestic is unconfirmed.
- No national-level average VBP price-cut statistic for CT or MRI. The figures above are provincial or media-compiled samples.
- No source was reviewed on whether Japan ever used domestic-preference procurement for medical devices. MOSS talks and US–Japan trade pressure were not researched, so the claim that Japan "never had" these tools rests on the absence of any evidence found, not on a documented comparison.
- I did not confirm when 财库〔2025〕19号 was announced, beyond the 2025-07-06 effective date.

### Inferences
- **Thesis test.** The claim "Siemens keeps the high end and does not join the price war" is half-right:
  - Right: Siemens' Imaging margin (26.5%) shows it is not chasing volume at a loss. It still leads PCCT, with >57% of the global market [MR].
  - Wrong: Siemens is cutting high-end prices (PCCT ~RMB 3m lower) and localizing high-end MR and CT components specifically to compete on cost under VBP. That is joining the price war through cost-down, not abstaining.
  - GE is doing both: cutting price at the top (the Zhejiang tender) and preparing to sell the China imaging business to a buyer that could pass as domestic in tenders. GE's move looks like the reverse of "keep the high end"; it is closer to Toshiba and Hitachi exiting.
- Defensible restatement: "Foreign majors retain share only at the very top (≥256-row CT, PCCT, top-end 3T MR) and fight there with localized cost bases. Mid/low-end share has already gone (73% domestic in ≤64-slice CT)."
- The GE sale rationale ("a Chinese owner could ... use in public tenders") is direct evidence that domestic-content rules, not only price, are driving foreign exit.

## Q6 (Part B). Which parts of the Japan story transfer to China in 2026, and which do not

### Takeaway
What transfers:
- Focused, listed pure-plays (UIH, Mindray) are structurally closer to Japan's winners than to Toshiba or Hitachi.
- Physician co-development and service networks matter: UIH already reports 44 overseas service sites and a ~14% service share.

What does not transfer:
- China has policy tools that Japan never had (based on what was found) and a far larger home market.
- Chinese challengers compete at the high end at home, not only on price.
- The recurring-revenue model that made Japan's winners profitable is being suppressed by VBP's bundled 5-year warranties.
- Chinese firms' route abroad is politically constrained, for example by the EU's €5m tender bar.

Net: China's imaging challengers can win their home market faster than Japan's imaging firms ever could. Whether they become profitable global leaders like Olympus or Terumo is not yet shown by the 2025–26 data.

### Inferences
Transfer scorecard (my synthesis):

| Japan-winner trait | Present in China's challengers? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|

| Invention or co-development lead | Partly: UIH firsts in total-body PET and whole-body 5T; PCCT behind Siemens | Partly transfers |

Further points:
- The Japanese imaging makers' failure mode — a parent sells a low-margin division — is less likely for UIH or Mindray. The China-specific failure mode is instead margin destruction by state price-setting at home, combined with exclusion abroad. That is closer to a "national champion with utility-like economics" than to an Olympus.
- For the investment thesis: China-for-China share gains are well evidenced. The jump to Olympus or Terumo-like global economics is not yet evidenced. The best leading indicators are:
  - UIH's overseas share moving from 25% toward 40% and beyond.
  - UIH's service share moving from 14% toward 20% and beyond.
  - UIH's gross margin holding through provincial PET/CT and PET/MR VBP rounds (by end-2026).
  - The fate of the GE China carve-out: if a Chinese-controlled buyer takes it, a new domestic-classified competitor appears at the high end.
