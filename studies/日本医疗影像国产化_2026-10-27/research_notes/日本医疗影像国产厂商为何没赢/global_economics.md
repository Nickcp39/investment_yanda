# Global competitive economics of medical imaging equipment, and why GE, Siemens and Philips (GPS) won while the Japanese makers did not (1975–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-27. Labels used below:
- **[PRIMARY]**: company filing, results release or exchange disclosure.
- **[MR]**: market-research estimate (Frost & Sullivan, GlobalData, Signify, Statista and others), usually seen through trade-press summaries.
- **[PRESS]**: trade or financial press.
- **[SELF-CLAIM]**: a vendor's own ranking claim.
- **UNVERIFIED**: no source was found in this session.

Comparability warning. The segment perimeters differ:
- GE HealthCare "Imaging" covers MR, CT, MI and X-ray. Ultrasound sits in its "AVS" segment.
- Siemens Healthineers "Imaging" excludes Advanced Therapies and Varian.
- Philips "Diagnosis & Treatment" (D&T) includes Image Guided Therapy (IGT) and ultrasound.
- Canon "Medical" includes in-vitro diagnostics (IVD) and healthcare IT.

## Q1. Global market shares by modality over time, verification of the "60%" and "65%" claims, and Canon's CT rank

### Takeaway
Every dated estimate found puts GPS in control of roughly 60–80% of global imaging-equipment revenue from the 1990s to today. The figure depends on the research firm's scope:
- 78% of new-equipment sales in 2012 (Frost & Sullivan).
- About 61–62% of "diagnostic imaging devices" in 2022–2024 (Statista, Research Nester).

On the specific claims:
- "GPS control over 60%" is consistent with the 2022–2024 market-research figures. Those are low-quality, paywalled sources.
- "GPS hold about 65% of global MRI" could not be found in any ITN article. The nearest dated MRI split (2011) gives GPS 58% and the two Japanese makers 21% (Toshiba 12%, Hitachi 9%). Treat the 65% figure as UNVERIFIED.

In CT, Toshiba/Canon is consistently ranked just outside the top three by third parties. Canon itself claims #3 globally, and Toshiba claimed #2 in 2016. Signify's 2024 report ranks Siemens, GE and Philips 1-2-3.

**Earlier history**
- [PRESS, c. 1993] Diagnostic Imaging reported:

### Inferences
- Across 1986–2024 the leadership set stayed fixed: GE and Siemens through the 1980s, Philips joining via the 1998–2001 roll-up. Only the order within the three changed, with Siemens currently #1 in CT and MRI by most accounts. The Japanese pair (Toshiba, Hitachi) peaked around 20% of MRI (2011) and roughly mid-teens of CT. In 2012 all three Japanese vendors together held only about 11% of all-modality new-equipment revenue (inference from the sources above).
- The "60%" claim is best read as a 2022–2024 all-modality figure from secondary market-research firms (61–62%). The higher 78% (2012) uses a narrower base (new equipment only, three named vendors). The difference is mostly about scope, plus the rise of Chinese and Korean vendors and the Fujifilm/Canon portfolios after 2016.
- From GlobalData's CT split, GPS held about 78–80% of 2016 CT revenue. Toshiba was "close behind" within the remaining ~20–22%, so it was probably in the low-to-mid teens. This is my inference, not a published number. It agrees with Toshiba's ~19% North American CT share in 2005.

## Q2. Revenue scale, R&D and operating margins: GPS vs Canon, Fujifilm, Toshiba and Hitachi

### Takeaway
In 2025 the imaging businesses of each GPS firm were about 2.5–4 times the size of Canon Medical. Their margins were higher by 4–15 points:
- Healthineers Imaging: about €13.2B revenue, about 21% adjusted EBIT margin.
- GE HealthCare: Imaging $9.2B at 9.6% plus AVS $5.4B at 22.0%.
- Philips D&T: €8.5B at 11.7% adjusted EBITA.
- Canon Medical: ¥581B (about $3.9B) at 5.6%, after a ¥165B goodwill write-down in 2024.

The Japanese units had mid-single-digit margins even before they were sold: Hitachi healthcare 5.7% in FY2014, Toshiba Medical about 6% on a standalone basis.

## Q3. The economics that decide winners: installed base and service, networks, M&A roll-ups, financing, software and bundling

### Takeaway
The winners make money on the installed base, not only on the box. Recurring service is 33–40% of GPS imaging revenue:
- Healthineers Imaging: about 40% (FY2017).
- GEHC: 34% of the group, about 39% excluding the PDx drug business.
- Philips D&T: about 37% recognised "over time".

GEHC's service gross margin (about 51%) is far above its product gross margin (about 35%). The Japanese conglomerate units ran at about 23% service share (Hitachi, FY2014).

GPS built global scale through decades of roll-ups:
- GE: Yokogawa JV 1982, Thomson-CGR 1987, Elscint 1998–2000, Amersham 2004.
- Philips: ATL 1998, ADAC 2000, Agilent HC 2001, Marconi 2001.
- Siemens: CTI 2005, Acuson, Varian.

They also run captive or partner financing arms and sell multi-year, multi-modality enterprise deals that bundle service and software.

## Q4. Price competition: what happens to a price-based entrant?

### Takeaway
The evidence suggests low price alone has not displaced GPS outside the entrant's home market. Three reasons:
1. Incumbents themselves move down-market. GE's China-built portable ultrasound cost about 15% of its previous low-end price, and sales rose from $5M to $278M between 2002 and 2008.
2. The installed-base and service annuity lets them match prices.
3. In Japan's cost-competitive market, GE's local JV launched low-priced high-field MRI.

Price-led entrants (Samsung Medison, Chinese vendors abroad) have built niches of about 6% or less in ultrasound, and about 22% of UI's own revenue comes from overseas. Profits concentrate in premium CT/MRI, where Siemens has gained share with photon-counting CT.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS, from HBR 2009, Immelt/Govindarajan/Trimble, "How GE Is Disrupting Itself"] GE's China ultrasound sales were only about $5M after a decade, because its scanners competed only at the top and 90% of hospitals could not afford them. A China team built a scanner priced at **$15,000, about 15% of GE's previous low-end ultrasound**, and sales rose from **$5M (2002) to $278M (2008)**. The portable units were then sold into the US and other rich markets for new uses (ambulances, operating rooms). Sources: [ReliablePlant reprint](https://www.reliableplant.com/Articles/Print/20157?id=20157); [HBR store](https://store.hbr.org/product/how-ge-is-disrupting-itself/R0910D)
- [PRESS, c. 1993] Cost containment pushed vendors to develop lower-priced systems and bring high-end features into cheaper CT and MRI. GE's Japanese subsidiary YMS introduced a **low-priced, high-field MRI for Japan's highly cost-competitive market**. Picker's 30–50% share of Japanese MRI eroded as Japanese makers competed on placements. Source: [Diagnostic Imaging](https://diagnosticimaging.com/view/ge-trimmed-time-tight-market)
- [ACADEMIC, Donzé 2018, reviewed by Otsubo 2019] Japan's market was shaped by "a large number of small hospitals that competed by investing in compact and cheap X-ray instruments". GE's strategy of sharing technology with Japanese partners beat Siemens's high-end, patent-protective approach before WWII. Source: [Bulletin of the History of Medicine review, Project MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/744917)
- [PRESS] United Imaging's Korean subsidiary (2019) competes mainly on lower price and after-sales service against "GE's high equipment and service costs". UI entered the US via UIH Solutions at RSNA 2018. Sources: [Medical Times](https://www.medicaltimes.com/Main/view.html?ID=1117337); [AuntMinnie](https://cdn.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/molecular-imaging/nuclear-medicine/article/15622250/united-imaging-breaks-into-us-imaging-market)
- [MR/broker] United Imaging 2024:
  - Overseas revenue RMB 2.3B (+35%), 22.0% of total.
  - Total revenue fell about 9.7%, and China's equipment market shrank 12.4% (IQVIA).
  - Gross margin about 50.4% in 1H24. The full-year gross margin is about 49% by my calculation from third-party data (revenue about RMB 10.3B, cost about RMB 5.29B).
  - Sources: [Pharnexcloud/CMBI](https://consult.pharnexcloud.com/report/detail/82939.html); [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/sha/688271/financials/income-statement/)
- [MR/broker] Mindray group gross margin was 66.3% in 1H24 (includes IVD). A secondary summary gives imaging gross margin of 59.1% in H1 2026, with international at 53% of revenue. Sources: [Quartr H1 2024](https://quartr.com/events/mindray-300760-h1-2024_FLrWuutw); [Quartr Q2 2026](https://quartr.com/events/shenzhen-mindray-bio-medical-electronics-co-ltd-300760-q2-2026_FLrWoyZQ)
- [PRIMARY] For comparison, GEHC Imaging-segment gross margin was about 34% in 2025, and Philips group gross margin about 45% (8,058 / 17,834). Sources: [GEHC 2025 AR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1932393/000193239326000025/a2025ars.pdf); [Philips 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/313216/000162828026009470/phg-20251231.htm)
- [PRESS] Samsung (Medison 2010, NeuroLogica CT in Jan 2013) was seen in 2013 as a possible challenger "starting to expand into even the more advanced modality sectors". Medison's global ultrasound share was about 6% in 2010. Sources: [DOTmed 2013](https://www.dotmed.com/legal/print/story.html?nid=20948); [Healthmanagement.org](https://healthmanagement.org/c/imaging/News/samsung-enters-ultrasound-market)
- [MR] Signify: Siemens expanded its CT leadership in 2023 through photon-counting CT, a premium, innovation-led segment. Source: [Signify](https://signifyresearch.net/?p=7679)

### Inferences
- **Gross margins do not track price positioning.** Chinese vendors that are positioned on price report *higher* consolidated gross margins (UI about 49–50%, Mindray imaging about 59%) than GEHC Imaging (about 34%). Possible reasons are product mix, domestic-market pricing, and cost structure (labour, localisation). Low price abroad therefore does not imply low margin at home. The gross-margin bases also differ (GEHC Imaging includes some service; UI and Mindray mix differs), so this is directional only.
- **Price leadership is a niche strategy.** Over 1985–2025 the price-led challengers (Toshiba and Hitachi in the West, Samsung Medison, now UI) each held single-digit to low-teens shares outside their home market. GPS kept about 60–80% overall. The incumbents' responses were local low-cost product lines (YMS in Japan, GE China ultrasound) plus service and installed-base economics. Profits sit in premium CT and MRI (photon-counting, 3T, AI reconstruction), where pricing is set by technology leaders rather than discounters.

## Q5. Japanese firms abroad: overseas revenue share and obstacles

### Takeaway
The Japanese imaging units were export-oriented: Hitachi healthcare was 62% overseas in FY2014, and Canon Medical was about 66% overseas in 2025, with the Americas 30%. They still never reached GPS-level service share, scale or margins.

Documented obstacles:
- A late and thin US/EU field organisation. Toshiba's US unit started in 1979, Europe in 1983, and it shifted resources to field sales and service in 1991.
- A "Japan-centric" orientation that Toshiba was still trying to leave in 2003.
- Low service share (23% at Hitachi).
- Parent conglomerates whose priorities lay elsewhere.

## Q6. Conglomerate capital allocation: Japan sold, the West spun off; valuation multiples

### Takeaway
Toshiba sold Toshiba Medical (2016) under duress. Its accounting scandal and Westinghouse losses had pushed its equity ratio toward 2.6%, and it auctioned its best asset. Canon paid ¥665.5B, about 1.6 times sales and about 38 times standalone operating profit (my calculation). Of that, ¥492B was goodwill, and Canon wrote off ¥165B of it in 2024.

Hitachi sold its imaging unit to Fujifilm (2019–21) for an enterprise value of ¥179B (about 1.25 times sales) under "selection and concentration". It said in its own disclosure that scale was becoming decisive.
