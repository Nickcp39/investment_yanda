## Q1. Toshiba Medical Systems (TMSC): firsts, financials, the Canon sale, and Canon Medical today

### Takeaway
Most of the AI's Toshiba technology claims hold up, with two nuances. Japan's first *domestically built* CT was Hitachi's (Oct 1975), not Toshiba's; Toshiba's 1975 CT was an imported/licensed EMI unit. And "1982 MRI" means Japan's first MRI *clinical case* (Toshiba, 1982); the first domestic *commercial* MRI was Toshiba's MRT-15A in 1983. The sale to Canon (¥665.5bn, agreed 17 Mar 2016, closed 19 Dec 2016) was driven by the parent's accounting-scandal capital crisis, not by a failing unit: TMSC was profitable at about a 6% margin. Under Canon the business is intact. Its 2025 sales were ¥580.6bn and operating profit ¥32.8bn (5.6%, a record), but Canon wrote off ¥165.1bn of the acquisition goodwill in 2024.

### Cited Findings
**Entry and product firsts**
- In 1975 Toshiba delivered Japan's first head CT, an EMI unit sold under an EMI sales agreement, to Tokyo Women's Medical University [P, company museum, snippet] — [Toshiba Mirai Kagakukan](https://toshiba-mirai-kagakukan.jp/a-guide/en/history-zone/ichigoki/ct.htm). The page itself returned 503; the text comes from the search snippet.
- The first CT in Japan was the EMI Mark 1 at Tokyo Women's Medical College (Aug 1975). Japan's first *domestically built* CT was a Hitachi CT-H 250 prototype installed at Fujita Health University Hospital in Oct 1975; its first clinical image was taken 16 Oct 1975 [P, participant university page] — [Fujita Health Univ. history (Katada)](https://www.fujita-hu.ac.jp/~kkatada/history_folder/history_01.html)
- The JIRA (Japan Medical Imaging & Radiological Systems Industries Assn.) CT chronology chart says the following [P, industry association]:
  - Toshiba's first CTs were EMI imports and licensed models: "CT-1010のライセンス生産" (licensed production of the CT-1010).
  - Toshiba's TCT-900S is labelled "世界初高速連続回転CT" (world's first high-speed continuous-rotation CT, 1-second scan).
  - Hitachi is labelled "国産一号機" (first domestic unit) and "国産初の全身用CT" (first domestic whole-body CT).
  - The source is [JIRA xrayct_pdf02.pdf](https://www.jira-net.or.jp/vm/pdf/xrayct_pdf02.pdf), corrected 2009-04-13.
- **Conflict on the whole-body CT claim:** Toshiba's own chronology lists the TCT-60A (1978) as Japan's first domestic whole-body CT, installed at the National Cancer Center [P, snippet]. JIRA labels Hitachi's CT-W series as "国産初の全身用CT". JIRA also labels the imported EMI 5000 (Fukushima Medical Univ.) as the first whole-body CT operating in Japan. Sources: [Toshiba Mirai Kagakukan](https://toshiba-mirai-kagakukan.jp/a-guide/en/history-zone/ichigoki/ct.htm); [JIRA chart](https://www.jira-net.or.jp/vm/pdf/xrayct_pdf02.pdf)
- MRI history from the memoir of ex-Toshiba researcher Katsumi Kose, published by JIRA [P, participant]:
  - Toshiba's whole-body MRI used a Bruker 0.15T four-coil air-core magnet. It was installed at Toshiba Central Hospital around March 1982.
  - The first good head images came in about May 1982.
  - The results were presented at the 2nd Japan Society of Magnetic Resonance in Medicine meeting in July 1982, and the session chair praised them as "日本で最初のMRIの臨床例の報告" (the report of Japan's first MRI clinical case).
  - The "first domestic commercial MRI", the MRT-15A, was released the following year (1983).
  - Source: [JIRA mri_pdf01-02.pdf](https://www.jira-net.or.jp/vm/pdf/mri_pdf01-02.pdf)
- Toshiba's chronology lists the 1983 MRT-15A as its first MRI and the 1985 TCT-900S slip-ring CT as a world first [P, snippet] — [search summary of Toshiba chronology](https://toshiba-mirai-kagakukan.jp/a-guide/en/history-zone/ichigoki/ct.htm). A separate source says the MRI product project started in Oct 1981 [S, snippet] — [innervision 2008](https://www.innervision.co.jp/01inner/2008/pdf/iv0803_02.pdf)
- Toshiba America Medical Systems unveiled the Aquilion ONE on 26 Nov 2007 at RSNA, billed as the first dynamic volume CT [P]:
  - 320 detector rows × 0.5 mm, 16 cm coverage.
  - Commercial availability was planned for summer 2008 after FDA clearance.
  - Toshiba announced the 1,000th unit in July 2015.
  - Sources: [Canon Medical US press release 2007-11-26](https://us.medical.canon/news/press-releases/2007/11/26/252); [Canon Medical (CA) press release](https://ca.medical.canon/News/PressRelease/Detail/46024-834)

**Market position (pre-sale)**
- Newswitch (Nikkan Kogyo) reports that TMSC's CT was No. 1 in Japan, that the president targeted global No. 1 share by FY2018, and that TMSC was "always in the global top 3" in CT, competing with GE and Siemens [S, snippet; article years not confirmed] — [newswitch p/7226](https://newswitch.jp/p/7226); [newswitch p/3896](https://newswitch.jp/p/3896)

- The 2024 goodwill impairment was ¥165.1bn. Canon's stated reasons:

  - Shrinking Russia business after the Ukraine war.
  - China's economic slowdown.
  - Worsening finances at Japanese hospitals.
  - Rising labour and parts costs.

### Inferences
- The Toshiba case is a **parent-company failure, not an imaging-business failure**. The unit was profitable (6.3% margin) and drew three serious Japanese bidders. Toshiba sold its best asset because the accounting scandal and later losses pushed equity toward zero. It remains a Japanese-owned business today.
- Since 2016 Canon has kept roughly the same sales scale (about ¥550–580bn, about $3.7–3.9bn), with margins of only 4–6%. The ¥165.1bn impairment shows Canon itself admits it overpaid against its plan. That is evidence of mid-tier economics, not elimination.

## Q2. Hitachi Medical (日立メディコ) / Hitachi Healthcare: products, pre-sale performance, sale to Fujifilm, what Hitachi kept

### Takeaway
Hitachi built Japan's first domestic CT (1975) and became "the open-MRI Hitachi" with permanent-magnet systems (AIRIS, 1995). It added Aloka ultrasound via a TOB (tender offer) announced in Nov 2010, fully absorbed its medical subsidiaries by 2016, then sold the whole diagnostic imaging business to Fujifilm. The sale was announced 18 Dec 2019 at a business value of ¥179bn and closed 31 Mar 2021, under Hitachi's "selection and concentration" toward digital/IoT. The AI's "about $1.6bn" is correct. Hitachi kept radiotherapy and particle therapy, which moved to Hitachi High-Tech in April 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Hitachi's CT-H 250 prototype was installed at Fujita Health Univ. in Oct 1975 and was Japan's first domestically built CT. Hitachi and Fujita kept co-developing until 1985 (CT-W2, CT-W3, CT-HF) [P] — [Fujita history](https://www.fujita-hu.ac.jp/~kkatada/history_folder/history_01.html)
- JIRA chart [P] — [JIRA](https://www.jira-net.or.jp/vm/pdf/xrayct_pdf02.pdf):
  - Hitachi = "国産一号機" (first domestic unit), "国産初の全身用CT" (first domestic whole-body CT), "国産初のR-R方式全身用CT" (first domestic rotate-rotate whole-body CT).
  - Hitachi built some CT as "米国Philips社向けOEM製品" (OEM product for Philips US).
  - Hitachi was also a Japanese distributor of Picker CT.
- Open MRI [S, trade magazine]:
  - AIRIS (0.3T) launched in 1995 as Hitachi's first open MRI.
  - Cumulative open-MRI shipments passed 5,000 units in September 2008.
  - The article calls Hitachi "オープンMRIの日立" (the open-MRI Hitachi).
  - Source: [innervision 2009-04](https://www.innervision.co.jp/01inner/2009/pdf/iv0904_hitachi01.pdf)
- Successor AIRIS Vento Plus and APERTO Lucent Plus launched in 2020 and are now sold under the Fujifilm brand [P, Fujifilm product page] — [Fujifilm AIRIS Vento](https://www.fujifilm.com/uk/en/healthcare/mri-and-ct/permanent-magnet-open-mri-system/airis-vento-v7)
- Aloka: on 8 Nov 2010 Hitachi Medical (holding 12.35%) announced a TOB to make Aloka a wholly owned subsidiary, with Aloka's board in favour. The company became Hitachi Aloka Medical [S] — [MAonline 2010-11-08](https://maonline.jp/articles/20101108a/print); [MONOist 2015](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/articles/1508/04/news009.html)
- Hitachi Medical buyout [P]:
  - Hitachi Ltd ran a TOB for Hitachi Medical from 14 Nov to 19 Dec 2013 at ¥1,800/share, reaching 94.44% of voting rights.
  - On 30 Jan 2014 it decided on a share exchange to take full ownership, targeted by about April 2014.
  - Source: [Hitachi/Hitachi Medical release 2014-01-30](https://www.hitachi.com/content/dam/hitachi/global/ja_jp/press/articles/2014/01/0130/f_0130.pdf)
- 2015–2016 [P, snippet]: on 21 Jul 2015 Hitachi announced it would absorb Hitachi Medical and Hitachi Aloka Medical. The method was changed to an absorption-type split, effective 1 Apr 2016. Manufacturing went into Hitachi Healthcare Manufacturing (the renamed Hitachi Medical) — [JPX disclosure 2015-07-21](https://www2.jpx.co.jp/disc/65010/140120150721455214.pdf); [JPX disclosure 2016-01-13](https://www2.jpx.co.jp/disc/65010/140120160113486818.pdf); [kitaishihon timeline](https://kitaishihon.com/company/6501/story)
- Sale to Fujifilm:
  - Announced 18 Dec 2019. Scope: CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound, EMR.
  - Business value ¥179.0bn (final price subject to adjustment).
  - The business had FY ended March 2019 sales of ¥143.2bn [S] — [MONOist 2019-12-19](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/articles/1912/19/news064_2.html); [newswitch](https://newswitch.jp/p/26116)
  - Hitachi expected about ¥110bn of restructuring gain in FY ended March 2021 [S, Reuters] — [Newsweek Japan/Reuters](https://www.newsweekjapan.jp/articles/-/147719)
- Hitachi's stated reason: EVP Nishino said "事業の選択と集中を一層進め、デジタルソリューション分野に力を入れる" (push selection and concentration further and focus on digital solutions). The same day Hitachi agreed to sell Hitachi Chemical to Showa Denko for about ¥494bn [S, Reuters] — [Newsweek Japan](https://www.newsweekjapan.jp/articles/-/147719)
- Fujifilm's stated rationale: little product overlap (X-ray and ultrasound being the main overlaps), and the chance to combine Hitachi hardware with Fujifilm's AI and PACS [S] — [newswitch](https://newswitch.jp/p/26116)
- Timing [P/S]:
  - The original target closing was 1 Jul 2020 [S] — [MONOist](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/articles/1912/19/news064_2.html)
  - On 18 Feb 2021 the absorption-type split agreement was signed and closing set for 31 Mar 2021, subject to clearances [P] — [JPX Hitachi 2021-02-18](https://www2.jpx.co.jp/disc/65010/140120210218466198.pdf); [JPX Fujifilm 2021-02-18](https://www2.jpx.co.jp/disc/49010/140120210218466271.pdf)
  - Completion was announced 31 Mar 2021 [S, snippet] — [Toyo Keizai 891723](https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/891723)
  - Fujifilm Healthcare Manufacturing (ex-Hitachi Medical manufacturing) became a 100% Fujifilm subsidiary in March 2021 [P, snippet] — [Fujifilm HCM history](https://www.fujifilm.com/hcm/ja/about/history)
- After the deal, on 1 Jul 2024 Fujifilm Healthcare Systems was absorbed and the company relaunched as "Fujifilm Medical Service Solutions" [P, snippet] — [Fujifilm FMSS message](https://www.fujifilm.com/fmss/ja/about/message)
- Fujifilm results [P/S]:
  - FY ended March 2025: Healthcare sales topped ¥1 trillion for the first time. Medical-systems growth came from medical IT and endoscopes [S] — [dempa-digital 2025-05-08](https://dempa-digital.com/?p=657933)
  - FY ended March 2026: Healthcare segment sales ¥1,098.9bn (+4.9%), operating profit ¥63.6bn (−20.3%). Medical Systems revenue was "前年度並み" (flat YoY). Growth drivers named were endoscopes, PACS "SYNAPSE" and IVD; China medical demand fell; CT and MRI are not named as drivers [P] — [Fujifilm FY2025 tanshin 2026-05-12](https://ir.fujifilm.com/ja/investors/ir-news/auto_20260508520206/pdfFile.pdf)
- What Hitachi kept:
  - Particle and radiation therapy stayed with Hitachi. It took over Mitsubishi Electric's particle therapy business (agreed Dec 2017, see Q3) and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries' X-ray radiotherapy business (agreed 2016) [P, snippet] — [Hitachi 2017-12-07](https://www.hitachi.com/ja-jp/press/articles/2017/12/1207/); [MHI 2016-08-04](https://www.mhi.com/jp/news/story/160804.html)
  - On 11 Oct 2023 Hitachi decided to transfer its Healthcare Business Unit to wholly owned Hitachi High-Tech, effective 1 Apr 2024 [P, snippet] — [Hitachi 2023-10-11 PDF](https://www.hitachi.co.jp/New/cnews/month/2023/10/f_1011.pdf); [nihon-ma](https://www.nihon-ma.co.jp/news/20231011_6501-35/)
  - A Hitachi High-Tech recruiting ad claims "世界シェア2位" (No. 2 worldwide) in particle therapy [S, self-promotion, unreliable] — [BizReach listing](https://www.bizreach.jp/job/view/6536489/)

### Inferences
- ¥179bn at about ¥109–110/$ (Dec 2019) is about $1.6bn, so the AI's USD figure is right. Against FY2018 sales of ¥143.2bn, the price is about 1.25× sales, versus about 1.6× for Toshiba Medical (¥665.5bn / about ¥405.6bn). This is consistent with Hitachi's imaging business being the lower-value asset.
- Hitachi's imaging business was sold to another Japanese company and still exists. Only the Hitachi brand left CT, MRI, X-ray and ultrasound; Hitachi stayed in particle therapy.

## Q3. Mitsubishi Electric particle-beam therapy: transfer to Hitachi

### Takeaway
This was not a "gradual divestment over the late 2010s–2020s". It was a single business transfer to Hitachi, agreed 7 Dec 2017 and planned to take effect April 2018, with the price undisclosed. Mitsubishi Electric was No. 1 in Japan (9 of 17 domestic facilities) but had no overseas orders.

### Cited Findings
- Joint release, 7 Dec 2017 [P] — [Hitachi press 2017-12-07](https://www.hitachi.com/ja-jp/press/articles/2017/12/1207/); [Mitsubishi Electric PDF](https://dl.mitsubishielectric.co.jp/news/2017/1207.pdf):
  - Scope: Mitsubishi Electric's particle therapy design, manufacturing, sales and maintenance, to be integrated into Hitachi in April 2018, subject to authority review.
  - Hitachi had orders from 13 hospitals in Japan, North America and Asia (8 operating) and more than 16,000 patients treated.
  - Mitsubishi Electric had supplied 9 of the 17 Japanese particle-therapy sites.
  - Rationale: market growth (more than 70 facilities worldwide, more than 10 new ones a year) and combining development resources. No price was disclosed.
- Trade-press details [S] — [MONOist 2017-12-27](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/spv/1712/27/news019.html); [newswitch 11535](https://newswitch.jp/p/11535):
  - Price undisclosed, estimated in the "数十億円規模" (tens of billions of yen) range.
  - About 100 employees transferred.
  - Production moved from Mitsubishi Electric's Power Systems Works (Kobe) to Hitachi Works (Hitachi City).
  - Mitsubishi Electric's orders were domestic only, and overseas expansion was its problem.

### Inferences
- The divestment was "one-shot, domestic consolidation". Particle therapy stayed Japanese-owned; it was consolidated under Hitachi, now Hitachi High-Tech.

### Gaps
- No primary closing announcement (April 2018) was retrieved.
- No financials for Mitsubishi Electric's particle-therapy unit were found.

## Q4. Shimadzu: did it make CT and MRI, when did it retreat, and what is it today?

### Takeaway
Shimadzu did make both CT (the SCT series from the 1970s) and MRI (the Magnex Epios line). It retreated from MRI outside Japan in Dec 1999 because of low volume, rising costs and an inability to keep pace with the technology. In multislice CT it distributed Marconi's MX-8000 (2001) and later outsourced the CT part of its PET/CT. Today its medical business is X-ray–centred, small and thinly profitable (about 3.8% margin in H1 FY2025). The "Harvard Business School case on a price war" could not be found; the known HBS CT case is about EMI.

### Cited Findings
- JIRA chart, Shimadzu section [P] — [JIRA](https://www.jira-net.or.jp/vm/pdf/xrayct_pdf02.pdf):
  - Own CTs SCT-100N (head), SCT-10N, SCT-200N, SCT-1000T through SCT-4800T (INTELLECT), plus "CTS" radiotherapy-planning CTs.
  - Labels: "世界初 放射線治療計画用CT" (world's first radiotherapy-planning CT) and "世界初 一般撮影可能2管球対応CT" (world's first dual-tube CT also usable for general radiography).
  - Distribution of imported ACTA (Pfizer) and Picker CTs.
  - 2001: "MX-8000 (LS, 4列マルチ, Marconi)", meaning Shimadzu sold Marconi's 4-row multislice CT.
- Shimadzu agreed to distribute Marconi's multislice CT alongside its own CT line [S, title/snippet] — [Medtech Insight](https://medtech.pharmaintelligence.informa.com/MT076217/Marconi-and-Shimadzu-in-CT-Computed-Tomography-distribution-deal)

YMS was established 1 Apr 1982 as a GE–Yokogawa JV, reportedly GE 51% / Yokogawa 49%, with GE moving to 75% in 1986 (secondary source). ## Q7. Other Japanese imaging makers missing from the AI's list

### Takeaway
The AI's list leaves out the areas where Japan *won* or kept strong positions: GI endoscopy (Olympus, Fujifilm, HOYA/Pentax, together about 90%+ of the world market), Canon's own X-ray and digital radiography lineage, and Fujifilm's X-ray and medical IT. Ultrasound was consolidated among Japanese firms rather than lost: Panasonic sold to Konica Minolta (2013), Aloka went to Hitachi and then Fujifilm, and Fujifilm bought SonoSite (2012). JEOL briefly made CT.

### Cited Findings
- Endoscopy:
  - Olympus, Fujifilm and Pentax hold over 90% of the world market, with Olympus at about 70% [S, snippet] — [nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/ja/features/c00513/)
  - Olympus' 2019 integrated report shows its GI endoscope share above 70%, with Fujifilm and HOYA at about 15–20% each [P, snippet] — [Olympus integrated report 2019](https://www.olympus.co.jp/ir/data/integratedreport/pdf/integrated_report_2019j_04.pdf)
  - Japanese firms hold "8 割近く" (close to 80%) of the world market for medical GI endoscopes [S, academic preprint, snippet] — [JST Jxiv preprint](https://jxiv.jst.go.jp/index.php/jxiv/preprint/download/689/2039/1857)
- Olympus put the gastrocamera into practical use in 1950, a world first [P, snippet] — [Olympus IR 2021](https://www.olympus.co.jp/ir/individual/pdf/ir_individual_2021_01.pdf)
- Canon's medical business traces to 1940, when it developed Japan's first domestic indirect X-ray camera [S, snippet] — [newswitch 4303](https://newswitch.jp/p/4303)
- Fujifilm acquired SonoSite (portable ultrasound, USA) in March 2012 and TeraMedica (medical IT, USA) in May 2015 [S, snippet] — [newswitch 4424](https://newswitch.jp/p/4424)
- In 2013 Panasonic Healthcare agreed to transfer its ultrasound diagnostic business to Konica Minolta [P, snippet] — [PHC HD 2013-07-26](https://www.phchd.com/jp/phc/news/2013/0726)
- JEOL (日本電子) appears in the JIRA chart with its own JXV-3A and JXV-3B electron-scan CTs; Asahi Medical and Mochida distributed imported CTs [P] — [JIRA](https://www.jira-net.or.jp/vm/pdf/xrayct_pdf02.pdf)
- Konica Minolta stayed in the bidding for Toshiba Medical until late in the process [S] — [DotMed](https://www.dotmed.com/news/story/29551)

### Inferences
- Japan's imaging industry did not die. It **consolidated from the electronics conglomerates (Toshiba, Hitachi) into the imaging and optics companies (Canon, Fujifilm)**. The 2015–16 Toshiba auction drew three Japanese optics and imaging bidders (Canon, Fujifilm, Konica Minolta), and Fujifilm then bought Hitachi's business. Where Japan led on a core optical technology (endoscopy), it still dominates.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) audited global share for endoscopy or ultrasound by company was found.
- The Fukuda Denshi and Konica Minolta ultrasound business trajectories after 2013 were not researched.
