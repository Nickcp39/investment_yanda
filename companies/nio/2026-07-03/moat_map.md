# Moat Map

## Moat candidates

### 1. Brand and community

NIO has a stronger premium identity than many Chinese EV startups. This can support pricing, retention, referrals, and service monetization.

Moat test: does NIO sustain premium ASP and retention while ONVO/FIREFLY expand, or does the brand architecture become diluted?

### 2. Battery swap network

Battery swap can reduce charging anxiety, improve convenience, support BaaS economics, and create a physical network advantage.

Moat test: station utilization, capex per station, service margin, third-party adoption, and customer willingness to pay.

### 3. Software / smart driving

NIO WorldModel and in-house chip work can improve user experience and reduce reliance on external stacks.

Moat test: actual feature adoption, safety record, take-rate, subscription revenue, and whether competitors match quickly.

### 4. Multi-brand platform

NIO / ONVO / FIREFLY can cover more price bands while sharing engineering and supply-chain costs.

Moat test: shared platform savings must exceed added complexity and marketing burden.

## Moat score

- Brand: medium.
- Swap network: potentially high, economically unproven.
- Software: medium, needs monetization proof.
- Manufacturing scale: medium-low versus BYD/Tesla.
- Cost leadership: low.

## Bottom line

NIO has differentiation, but not yet a proven economic moat. The closest moat is battery swap plus premium service ecosystem. The concern is that this moat may be expensive to maintain and hard to monetize at scale.
