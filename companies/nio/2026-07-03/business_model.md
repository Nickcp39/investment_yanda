# Business Model

## Revenue engine

NIO earns revenue mainly from vehicle sales, then layers on other sales from parts, accessories, after-sales vehicle services, power solutions, financing services, used cars, technical services, and subscription/service products.

The important shift is that NIO has moved from one premium brand into a three-brand structure:

- NIO: premium EVs, brand halo, executive / premium SUV focus.
- ONVO: broader family-oriented premium market.
- FIREFLY: smaller smart high-end EVs, likely lower ASP and broader reach.

## Unit economics question

The bull case needs the following chain to hold:

deliveries up -> factory scale improves -> vehicle margin holds -> service/power losses narrow -> SG&A/R&D leverage improves -> GAAP profit and cash flow become durable.

The weak link is not delivery volume. The weak link is margin durability after ONVO/FIREFLY mix increases and after competitors respond with price cuts.

## Good signs

- Q1 2026 vehicle margin reached 18.8%.
- Q1 2026 gross margin reached 19.0%.
- Other sales margin improved, according to management, helped by services and community-related businesses.
- R&D and SG&A were down sharply YoY in Q1 2026, reflecting cost discipline and organizational optimization.
- Liquidity of RMB48.2B provides runway.

## Bad signs / questions

- GAAP net loss persisted in Q1 2026.
- Q1 was down meaningfully from Q4 2025 on deliveries and revenue.
- Q2 delivery result missed guidance, even though it grew YoY.
- ONVO and FIREFLY made up about 46% of June deliveries. This can be healthy scale, but it may also pressure ASP and vehicle margin.
- Battery swap and power infrastructure may require high utilization to be economically attractive.

## What matters next

Q2 2026 financials should answer whether the Q2 volume step-up created economic profit or only headline growth. Watch vehicle margin, gross margin, operating cash flow, inventory, payables, and the implied ASP/mix.
