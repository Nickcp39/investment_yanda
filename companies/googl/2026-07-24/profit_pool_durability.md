## The two offsetting forces this quarter

**IMPROVED — incremental-moat evidence:**
- Cloud swung from a single-quarter 32.9% margin (which June flagged as unproven) to **35.6% across a second quarter, with op income tripling to $8.8B and a $513.9B backlog**. The "can post-AI-capex incremental capital earn high returns?" test — the central M3 question — got its first real *yes* from Cloud. This is genuine.
- Search durability (+17%) confirms the existing moat (habit + distribution + auction liquidity) is intact through two years of AI-answer pressure.

**WORSE — capital-allocation discipline:**
- Capex guidance RAISED to **$195–205B** (2027 higher) into a quarter where **FCF went negative** — and management STILL disclosed **no ROI hurdle rate** (CFO Ashkenazi's "as long as we see an attractive return" is a stated philosophy, not a framework or a number). K-C remains unresolved.
- **52.7% founder voting power** unchanged, now more salient: with buybacks at $0, $49.6B of new equity + preferred issued, and debt doubled, minority holders have *no lever* to demand capex discipline if the returns disappoint. The dual-class structure is amplifying an un-checked capex bet.
- Alphabet-level shared AI R&D loss widened to −$5.8B/qtr; Other Bets loss −$1.8B.

## Net

The Cloud proof points (incremental returns showing up) roughly offset the worse capital-allocation optics (more capex, no hurdle, no external check). Durability of the *existing* franchise is Strong and better-evidenced; the *incremental* moat is now partially — but not fully — proven, and it is being funded in a way (dilution + leverage + negative FCF) that a disciplined external board might not sanction. Operator grade holds ~28/40 (founder-fit and long-termism strong; capital-return discipline and governance-check weak).

Signal: **+1** (held). The profit pool is deep and the incremental-return evidence improved, but the absence of a capex-return framework plus the governance-lever gap keep this below a Confirmed +2.
